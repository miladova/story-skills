# The Home Army

The Underground state's army: some three hundred thousand sworn in the Generalgouvernement, a command kept in a house on a street it does not name, a counter-intelligence section that keeps its files in handwriting, a sabotage directorate that assassinates German officials and pays for it in reprisals, and a vast unpaid civil apparatus of nurses, couriers, forgers, printers and teachers.

It is the story's institution and it is not gentle. It asks for everything, gives rank in secrets, and treats a person as a resource with a service life. Its leaders know that the Rising they are preparing for will be fought without help at the right moment and that it will cost a generation, and they intend to fight it anyway — which is the historical fact the book must hold without either cheering or condescending.

The section that owns Krystyna is counter-intelligence: file names, German order of battle, the garrison's mood, the names of traitors. It is the part of the Underground that loses the most people and speaks of it the least. People in it are professional liars the way she is, and that is why they are the only people who can hold her.
