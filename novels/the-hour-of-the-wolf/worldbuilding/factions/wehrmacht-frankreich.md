# The German military administration

The Wehrmacht's administrative machine in the occupied west, and after February 1944 the garrison machinery in Warsaw. Not the SS; not friends of the SS either. Its officers are professionals who consider themselves soldiers rather than butchers, which the book treats as a distinction without a difference: the requisition orders, the labour round-ups, the reprisal shootings and the trains all pass across their desks, and every man in this novel has signed one of them.

Its internal weather in 1944 is dread of the eastern front and suspicion of Berlin, and its officers talk about the war the way men talk about weather that is going to ruin the harvest — a thing to be survived, at other people's expense.

The organisation's one great weakness, and the reason the story exists: it is full of paper. Somebody types everything, somebody copies everything, and the copies are handled by women who are assumed to be furniture.
