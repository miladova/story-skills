# The Polish intelligence service in France

The remnant of the Polish General Staff's intelligence work in France after June 1940: two officers in a flat in the 15th arrondissement, a priest, a dressmaker, a forger, four couriers, and a line of reports that goes out through Spain, Switzerland and the Polish government's channels in London, and comes back as instructions two and three months old. It is not a grand conspiracy; it is six people with a typewriter and a very good sense of what matters.

Their tradecraft comes out of 1930s Warsaw: memory over paper, patience over daring, the absolute refusal to be interesting. Wiktor's rules are its catechism, and Krystyna is its masterpiece — a Polish agent who has never once been inside a Polish institution in France, because she was built outside it deliberately, for the day they would need somebody the Germans could not find in a card index of known faces.

It disappears as an organisation in this book: rolled up section by section between January and March 1944. Its legacy is entirely her.
