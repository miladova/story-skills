# The Stankiewicz signet ring

A silver signet, worn flat and shiny across the bezel: a greyhound's head above three willow leaves, the crest of a family that has been paying for its name for a century. Her father's, given to her in 1940 in the hallway while the Germans were carrying the sofa out, and worn on a cord inside her blouse from that day; nobody in Paris ever sees it.

It is the single object that can prove who she is and the single object that can hang her, and the book tracks it the way a spy story tracks a codebook: it is nearly found in a dressing room in Paris, it is left in a glove in a Warsaw flat during a search, and in Part Three it changes hands once, in the last ten minutes of the Colonel's life.

## Payoff Rule

The ring is not a symbol of nobility. It is a piece of identification, and in the Warsaw of 1944 owning one is a way of being shot slower.
