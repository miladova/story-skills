# The Colonel's briefcase

Black leather, brass corners, three inches too big for its frame, carried by Emil Rutz and left on chairs in rooms where it should not be. Inside: duty rosters, railway timetables, the weekly intelligence appreciation from Berlin, and — the item the whole book is written around — the file of papers that maps the German garrison's dispositions in Warsaw and the order that will destroy the city after the Rising is put down.

Its contents move three times: stolen copies in Paris, photographed by memory in Warsaw, and finally carried out of the German quarter by a woman in a fur coat at five in the morning with the papers in the lining, which is what the story's last theft is and how it is done.
