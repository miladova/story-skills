# Hôtel Lutetia, boulevard Raspail

The Abwehr's Paris address: white stone, glass canopy, a lobby with palm trees that were watered until 1940 and are now dust. Military intelligence, its Polish desk on the second floor, its heating inadequate.


The Abwehr's Paris house. It is a strange building for a secret service: wide windows, a garden entrance, and a lobby where a man can watch who comes in without appearing to.

The Polish desk there reads Warsaw newspapers three weeks old and interrogates emigrants. It is where Phillip Brandt is given his first real work. It is also, by the winter of 1943, a place where officers have begun to be careful about what they say about Berlin.
