# Wierzbów

The family estate: a manor house of one storey and a half with a white portico and a leaking wing, a birch wood, a ditch, the boundary path along it, a kitchen garden, a well, the home farm, and four hundred and ten thousand zlotys of debt against the beet land that the syndicate from Poznań had bought and put their dogs on.

It is the place the Stankiewiczes lost before the war that then took everything else: the sale agreed in Warsaw on the eleventh of August 1939, the notice to quit for the middle of October, the general mobilisation, and the whole transaction sitting in a folder in a country that stopped existing on the first of September.

For Krystyna it is the last summer: the summer of 1938, the partridges, the leaves coming down, the boundary path along the ditch, and the four things said on the walk there by a boy who was going back to Berlin and a girl who had already decided to marry a man who would stay in Warsaw. Every chapter that looks back looks back to this estate and to the hour at its well, and every rule she keeps in the war is a rule she first learned on this ground.
