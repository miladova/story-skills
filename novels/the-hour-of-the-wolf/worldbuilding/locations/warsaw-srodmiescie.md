# Śródmieście

The centre of Warsaw: the Marszałkowska and the Nowy Świat, the courtyards and the cellars, the burned streets of 1944 and the barricades built out of cobblestones and tram cars. By September 1944 eleven streets of it are held by the Polish Home Army and the rest is a corridor between the German posts and the ruins of the ghetto.


The middle of the city, and the district in which most of this book's Warsaw happens: the flat above the cobbler's shop on the Zielna, the cellar on the Śliska in which the section keeps its ledger, the dressing station on the Zielna, the offices of the counter-intelligence section, and the rooms with the maps.

It is the district that holds through the rising longest, the district into which the canal crossings come out at the mouth on the Warecka Street, and the district in which the files of the Polish Home Army are burned in the courtyards on the second of October 1944.
