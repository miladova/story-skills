# The villa on Frascati Street

A white villa with a garden wall, three doors from the corner of Bracka Street: requisitioned from a Jewish family in 1940 whose name is still on the brass plate they were told to unscrew and could not. The Colonel's Warsaw house, with a safe in the study and a cellar full of someone else's wine.


Warsaw's German quarter curls around the Ujazdowski Park and the aleja Szucha; requisitioned villas, German-only cafés, sentries at the corners, and the Polish population passing through it on the far pavement with their eyes on the ground, like people walking past dogs.

The villa's nameplate still reads *Zaleski*, in brass, on a wall painted German grey. The safe stands behind a lithograph of the Lilpop works at Burakowska Street — a lithograph the Colonel liked in a shop and bought legally with an occupation certificate.
