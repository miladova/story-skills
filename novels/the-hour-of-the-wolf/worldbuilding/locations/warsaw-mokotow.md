# Mokotów

The southern district, up the Puławska and the Madalińskiego: the long streets of pre-war tenements, the gardens, the barracks, the tram depot, and the strong-points of the Polish Home Army's southern group in the rising.

In the book it is the district of the crossings and of the last cellar: the canal mouth at the top of the city that the crossing of the first and second of September 1944 comes out at, the ruined streets that a man on crutches can be hidden in for nine days of the autumn, and the cellar on the Madalińskiego Street of four metres by seven, with a brick vault and a well, in which the man who has hunted them all year is finally shot in a doorway.

It falls later than the centre and holds a month longer than the Old Town; and when the city is given up, it is the Mokotów that the last of the Home Army's paper is carried out of, in a coat that has already carried the map of the destruction of Warsaw through a transit camp.
