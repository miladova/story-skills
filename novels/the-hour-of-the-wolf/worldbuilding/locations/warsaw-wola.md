# Wola

The working district west of the centre: the gasworks on the Wolska, the tram lines and the tram sheds, the metal trades of the Burakowska, the churches of a parish of factory hands, and street after street of four-storey tenements built in the twenty years before the war for the people who worked in them.

The Brandts' flat is here — two rooms and a kitchen on the third floor of a corner building near the gasworks, taken cheap in 1940 after the house on Hoża Street was requisitioned, because Albrecht Brandt could walk to his works from it — and so is the small address on the Wola side at which a woman who does papers for the Home Army can be found by the people who are permitted to know it.

It is the district from whose direction the rising's first firing is heard, four minutes before five in the afternoon on the first of August: a rattle of small arms, flat and fast, from the north-west, half a kilometre away from the German quarter, which in a house full of officers could have been a lorry with a bad engine. By seven o'clock that evening the smoke stands up over it in two columns.

And it is the district in which, on the fifth and sixth of August 1944, the population is taken out of the houses and shot in the courtyards: men, women and children, in their own yards, in front of their own walls, without a list and without a question, by the units brought in for the purpose. The German papers of that week call it a clearing of the district. The order comes first to clear the streets, then to collect the population in the courtyards and at the assembly points, then the movement order for the civilian columns to the transit camp at Pruszków — and under the three of them the instruction that is written down nowhere and that every man in those columns understands, which is that the population of the Wola is to disappear. Albrecht Brandt is killed in it, with the neighbours, in the courtyard of his own works; his wife is taken in the cellar with the other families of the building, and is put out of the city with them in October; and on the sixth of August a man in Polish civilian clothes with a bag of dressings walks in through the ruins of the ghetto and takes nineteen people out of the district before dark.
