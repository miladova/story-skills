# Wola

The working district west of the centre: the gasworks on the Wolska, the tram sheds, the Burakowska and the Wolska running out towards the woods of the west, the factories of the metal trades, the churches of a parish of factory hands, and street after street of four-storey tenements built in the twenty years before the war for the people who worked in them.

The Brandt flat is here: two rooms and a kitchen on the third floor of a corner building near the gasworks, taken cheap in 1940 after the house on Hoża Street was requisitioned, because Albrecht Brandt could walk to his works from it. The smoking chimneys are the first thing seen from the windows in the morning, and the sound of the works is the sound of the day.

It is the district in which the German garrison's warehouses stand and the district in which the Home Army holds nothing at all on the first day of the rising, because the units that were to take it were not permitted to fight: the orders to a Polish boy in Wola on the first of August were to go to the collection point, not to the barricade.

And it is the district in which, on the fifth and sixth of August 1944, the population is taken out of the houses and shot in the courtyards by the units brought in for the purpose — men, women and children, without a list and without a question, in their own yards, in front of their own walls. The German papers of that week call it a clearing of the district. The Polish word for what was done there is not a word that this book can use twice.
