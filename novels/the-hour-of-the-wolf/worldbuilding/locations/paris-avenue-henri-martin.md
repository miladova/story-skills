# The House on the avenue Henri-Martin

A requisitioned hôtel particulier: three floors, a piano nobody plays, a garden with a statue of a stag, blackout curtains that cost a Frenchman his whole stock of velvet. Three Germans live here — the Colonel, his orderly, and, at the top of the house, the mistress.


Her address, and the room where the book begins: the Colonel's bedroom on the second floor, a bed with a headboard of grey silk, a writing desk with a blotter she has learned to read, and a wardrobe in which her own clothes hang between his uniforms like a thing held hostage.

The house has a service door onto a side street, a maid who sleeps in the basement, a sentry box at the gate that is occupied at night, and one broken step in the back stairs that every one of them has learned to skip without thinking.
