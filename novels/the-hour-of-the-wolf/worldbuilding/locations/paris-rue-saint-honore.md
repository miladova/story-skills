# The Polish Mission, rue Saint-Honoré

A small church with a Polish chaplain and a door that opens onto the commercial bustle of the rue Saint-Honoré: workers' coats, blackout paper still on the windows, a smell of candles and wet wool.


The place where she takes what she steals. On Thursdays and Sundays there are Poles here, and the chaplain's office has a screen, a stove, and a stack of devotional pamphlets from a press in Toulouse; on Tuesdays and Fridays it is empty enough for a woman in good boots to kneel in a side chapel for twenty minutes without anyone finding it remarkable.

The second and third pews on the left are never used. From there the chapel of Our Lady is out of sight of the street door and directly under the bell. The word goes: when the bell is rung twice, the courier has been and gone.
