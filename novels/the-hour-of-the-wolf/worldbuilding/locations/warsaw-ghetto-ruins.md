# The ruins of the ghetto

A brick desert. Sixteen square kilometres of rubble and burnt courtyards, the odd chimney standing, the stumps of walls, the smell that gets into wool and does not come out. Officially closed. Actually a road, a shortcut, a grave and an operational no man's land.


The ghetto rose in April 1943 and was burned to the ground through May; by 1944 the Germans keep guards on the edge of it, the Poles walk through it only where a gap has been opened by agreement with the darkness, and the black market uses the cellars because nobody follows anyone into that part of the city.

The book does not use the ruins as a metaphor. It is where the szmalcownik waits, where the executions are held, where two characters are taken for the last time, and where a whole murdered population is present in the bricks without anybody in the story being permitted a speech about it. What the story does say aloud is that there is no part of it that Krystyna can stop.
