# Hôtel Majestic, avenue Kléber

The Militärbefehlshaber in Frankreich: corridors of green baize, a marble stair, chandeliers with bulbs missing and clerks with card indexes, the smell of coffee substitute and floor polish. The heart of the occupation, and the most heavily guarded building in France.


Where the German administration of France is housed: departments for labour, food, transport, justice, and—up on the third floor, past two sentries and an appointments clerk who has never once been bribed—the counter-intelligence section to which Oberst von Hagen is attached.

Krystyna has been inside it four times, always on the Colonel's arm and always as furniture: enough to learn which corridor smells of what, who salutes whom, and that a woman who looks bored is never asked to leave a room.

## Details To Keep True

- The order of the blue carpet and the parquet: the German personnel are forbidden to slide on it in the presence of officers, and the soldiers do it anyway when they think nobody hears.
- The archive clerk, Fräulein Nagel, types with two fingers and keeps the carbon copies; the carbon is the reason the network wants the Majestic's in-tray and not its out-tray.
- German officers' visitors sign a ledger by the door. The names in it are worth more than money and cannot be photographed, only memorised.
