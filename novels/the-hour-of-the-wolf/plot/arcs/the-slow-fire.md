# The Slow Fire

## Setup
Two children of the same city, separated in September 1939 by a gate and a piece of politeness: Phillip came to the door of the house on Mokotowska Street with a plan to get the family out through the German lines, and Jerzy Stankiewicz threw him into the street for speaking German in his doorway. Krystyna watched him go from an upstairs window and has never known what it was about that afternoon that she was too proud to say. The reader gets this in chapter 6, intercut with the present, in which a man in a captain's uniform has just said her real name in a corridor of the Hôtel Majestic.

## Rising Action
The love is built entirely out of risk and almost no touch. He carries a file out of the Lutetia in his coat; she brings him the AK's conditions; they meet in a cold flat, a church, the lee of a ruined ghetto wall; the first kiss is delayed past the point where the reader wants it, and when the physical part of the love begins in Part Two it is deliberately set against the Colonel's appetite - slow, quiet, careful, absurdly tender, in a room with a candle and strangers' photographs on the sideboard, in a building where four other families could hear a dropped cup. Nothing in their story is grand, and it has to cost as much as the grand version would.

## Climax
The climax of the love is not a night; it is a decision. In chapter 16 the AK gives her a choice between killing him and controlling him, and she puts her hand on the thing she has never asked anyone for: she vouches for him, which puts his life and hers on the same sheet of paper. In the Rising, the love becomes work - he fights on a barricade, he is registered as a Home Army source under a pseudonym, he is nearly shot by his own side, and she has to hold the whole of it and still bring him out alive, or be responsible for his death.

## Resolution
They are not allowed an ending; they are allowed a possibility. At the capitulation he stays in the ruins among the people who will live in the rubble for three months, and she walks out with the column because that is what the Underground asks of her and because it is the only way either of them lives. The epilogue pays it: one year later, in a city that has fewer buildings than people, the thing they said on the road out of Warsaw - *in the spring* - turns out to have been a promise and not a farewell.
