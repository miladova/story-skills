# The Hunt

## Setup
There is a leak in the German administration in France. The file, opened in the autumn of 1943, gives it the name *Fräulein* - a woman, somewhere inside the German apparatus, whose product is being walked out of the Hôtel Majestic at a rate of one document a fortnight. Oberst von Hagen is a very good counter-intelligence officer, has broken four networks, and is hunting the leak with the same appetite with which he thinks he owns his mistress.

## Rising action
Kalbach's method is recurrence, and recurrence is the danger: he re-interviews the concierges, the dressmakers, the orderlies, the wives, and writes down what does not fit. The network loses three points of contact in Paris. Kalbach's chart grows until the Colonel's household is one of four rows on it, and the reader sees the row before Krystyna does. In Warsaw the hunt changes species: the leak is no longer a document thief but a woman in the German quarter with a Polish family, and von Hagen - humiliated by a disinformation operation the AK runs through her - turns from hunter to personal avenger.

## Climax
Chapter 23, the last night. He has her in his house at the end of September 1944 with the city burning on three sides of it, and the question on the table between them - not *are you a spy* but *is any of it true* - is unanswerable in either direction, and everything that happens in that study happens inside the answer.

## Resolution
He is dead and she is out of the house with the papers, and the hunt does not stop: Kalbach's investigation survives its hunters, and the last hunter dies on his knees in a cellar in Mokotów with a Home Army warrant officer's pistol behind his ear. The hunt's real payoff is the reader's realisation that the Germans were never the only ones hunting her; the Underground was too.
