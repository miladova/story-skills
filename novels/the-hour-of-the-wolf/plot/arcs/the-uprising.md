# The Uprising

## Setup
The Red Army is coming and the Underground knows what comes with it: a Soviet-backed committee already sitting in Lublin, the memory of Katyn, and the certainty that whoever takes Warsaw will write the next fifty years. The Home Army's answer is Operation Tempest, and its last act will be fought inside the city itself. Everything the counter-intelligence section does in Part Two is in service of a single question: when the Rising starts, where will the German garrison be, what will it have in the city, and what will it do to the city when it loses.

## Rising Action
Krystyna's stolen documents stop being about saving lives and start being about the timing of a battle: the order of battle, the ammunition dumps, the Panzer units pulled back to the Vistula, the state of the bridges, the garrison's casualty lists, the exact date on which the Germans intend to move the bulk of their staff out of Warsaw. In exchange the AK gives her the one thing she wants and cannot ask for: they take Phillip on as an asset, which means that if the two of them die, they die on the same page of somebody's file.

## Climax
1 August at five in the afternoon, and then sixty-three days of it: Wola and the murders there, the Old Town in flames, the sewers, the capitulation signed in a factory at Ozarow, the population driven out, the city pulled down street by street after them while the world watches and does nothing. The book does not manage this material; it survives it at eye level, one street and one cellar and one dispatch at a time.

## Resolution
The Underground loses. It is not rescued, not reinforced, and not thanked. What it wins is that the Rising happened, and that after it the city was a ruin full of people who had been in it, and the book's last image is one of those people standing in a grave that used to be a street, one year later, still alive, still Polish, still there.
