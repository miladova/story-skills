# The Mask

## Setup
Krystyna is two people and has stopped noticing. Marion Herschlag sings at German parties, forgets what officers tell her, and sleeps with the man who signs the requisition orders for her city; Krystyna carries what she steals to a priest on the rue Saint-Honoré and does not permit herself a single sentence of self-pity. The mask is not a lie she tells; it is a machine she operates, and Part One shows the machine being maintained: the smile, the forgetfulness, the perfume, the way she lets him find her stupid.

## Rising Action
The machine's price becomes visible. Céline Marchand is arrested on paper that Krystyna made; Odile Boin dies at Fresnes without giving her up; Wiktor is taken; the family in Warsaw has been told that their daughter is dead. In Part Two every street corner carries a face that might know her, and the two Poles who do know - her brother and her sister - must lie for her, which teaches her that the mask is not protecting other people from the work; it is protecting other people from her.

## Climax
The mask fails in the ways it was designed to fail. She is seen: by Phillip in Paris, by her father's own eyes in Warsaw, by the szmalcownik in the ruins of the ghetto, and finally by Kalbach, whose file has her name in it one line above the word *unverified*.

## Resolution
At the end the mask is not torn off. She leaves the city wearing it, because it is the only face that will get her through the queues at Pruszków, and the epilogue's question is not who she really is but whether she can put it down now that she no longer has to hold it up.
