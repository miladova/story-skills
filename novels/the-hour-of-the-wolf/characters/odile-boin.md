# Odile Boin

## Appearance

Fifty-five, small, iron-backed, with an eye like a set square and hands that have been pinning cloth since she was fourteen. She wears good grey wool from the pre-war years and a thimble she has owned since 1919. Chalk on her cuffs, always. The atelier on the rue de Miromesnil has three windows, one mirror, and a floor that creaks in the same place every time, which is how she knows when someone has come in.

## Personality & Traits

A French bourgeois professional who has never in her life called the occupation a moral question in company, and who has been forging Kennkarten, ration coupons and identity papers for the Polish and Jewish underground in her workroom since 1941, because the alternative would be to become someone she would have to live with. She is sardonic, exacting about work and honest about cloth, kind to Krystyna in a way that has nothing to do with politics, and appallingly calm.

She has one son, Émile, twenty years old, hidden in the Loire with a cousin, and she will not say where he is, not because she is heroic but because the reflex has become the structure of her mind.

## Voice & Speech Patterns

Familiar French with a workshop's bluntness, calls every young woman *chérie* whether she likes her or not. She talks about paper the way other people talk about horses: weight, sizing, wear. She tells the truth about fit and lies about everything else.

> "Stand still, chérie. If the Germans arrest you for standing badly, there is nothing I can do."

## Character Arc

She is the first cost of the book, and her death happens offstage in a way that leaves nothing for Krystyna to attend: no grave, no body, only a workroom with chalk on the table. The reader should feel that as the shape of the war, not as a plot beat.
