# Bruno Kalbach

## Appearance

Thirty-eight, and looks like a bank clerk who runs several miles before breakfast. Medium, spare, colourless: brown hair with no grey in it, a face that takes photographs badly, spectacles he cleans with the corner of his handkerchief while he waits for an answer. He wears the SD uniform as though it were a laboratory coat. One shoulder sits a little lower than the other, from a childhood fall, and he has learned to sit square to hide it.

He carries three notebooks and no weapon he has ever drawn.

## Personality & Traits

The bureaucratic face of terror. He is not sadistic — his cruelty is investigative, and indifference is his method. He has never shouted at a prisoner; he has never needed to. He does not lie if the truth can be arranged to do the work.

His method is exhaustive recurrence: he re-interviews, he re-checks, he re-reads. When two small facts are both true and cannot both be, he goes and stands between them until something gives, and his patience is the most frightening thing about him. He believes in a German Europe with the tranquillity of a man who believes in table salt.

He is genuinely, unostentatiously good at his job, and his job has never once made him a happy man — a detail the book should let exist without explanation.

**Strengths:** Cross-referencing; interviews; the memory of a man who catalogues; immune to charm
**Flaws:** Contempt for passion, which is why he fails to understand what he is looking at; no imagination for anything but evidence
**What he misses:** That a woman can be in a German bed and love a German man and betray both. He files the possibility under *improbable: sentiment*.

## Motivations & Goals

**Primary:** Identify the source in the German administration whom the Paris files call *Fräulein*, and end the network behind her.
**Internal:** To be right, in writing, in a file other men will read
**Blind spot:** He is looking for a spy; the spy's weapon is domestic and it is in the room with him

## Voice & Speech Patterns

Flat, exact, third-person plural for institutions — *we note*, *we have arranged* — and singular for facts: *the ink is from the Hotel Bristol*; *the glove was bought in Warsaw, but the wear on the thumb is from a bridle, not a handbag.* He finishes other people's sentences in his head and then waits for them to finish them aloud. He says *in time* whenever he means *you will answer me eventually*.

> "In time we shall know how many of the Colonel's household could write German. In time we shall know which of them wrote this."

## How He Works (For Drafting)

- He builds a chart, not a theory: names down the left, capabilities across the top, and marks in ink. The reader sees the chart in chapter 15 and understands that Krystyna's name is one row of it.
- His leverage is domestic: housekeepers, orderlies, dressmakers, concierges. He interviews the servants of the German elite the way another man reads the papers, because servants know the hours.
- Nobody is arrested without a file, so his appearances are warnings, not incidents — the terror is that he leaves and writes down what you said.
- He never announces what he suspects, so the reader deduces from his questions. By chapter 20 the reader should be ahead of Krystyna on exactly one thing: Kalbach is already inside the door.
