# Céline Marchand

## Appearance

Thirty-nine, blonde by chemistry, quick-eyed, always cold, always in the same two good suits and a sable she bought at a good price from a Jew in 1942 and has told herself was a favour. She wears too much lipstick and laughs too loudly in restaurants where officers turn round, and she is the only person in Part One who is warm to Marion Herschlag for no reason at all.

## Personality & Traits

A manufacturer's wife with a taste for the war economy and no politics whatsoever beyond the price of things. She is generous, indiscreet, uncurious about where her money comes from, and possessed of an eye for other women's clothes and other women's accents that makes her more dangerous than she has any right to be. She is the reader's spy in the German drawing rooms: everything the reader learns about Marion's social cover arrives through her gossip.

## Voice & Speech Patterns

Parisian, fast, confiding, hyperbolic; says *ma chérie* and *mon Dieu*; discusses politics as a shade of hat.

> "Marion, listen, the oysters were flown in. Flown. And the general's wife wore a hat like a birth control device. Don't look at me like that, I am not the one who is sleeping with a Prussian."

## Function In The Book

She asks the dangerous question about the accent in chapter 2, and in chapter 7 Krystyna gives her to Kalbach's investigation to buy her own life, which is the first act in the novel that the reader cannot forgive her for, and should not. She is mentioned twice afterwards: once as a file number, and once, at the very end, in a list of names that Krystyna recites in the rubble, which is the closest thing to a prayer in this book.
