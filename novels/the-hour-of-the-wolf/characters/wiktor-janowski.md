# Wiktor Janowski

## Appearance

Fifty-two, a Polish intelligence officer who has been abroad since 1939 and looks it: a French coat worn through at the elbows, a bad leg from a training accident, a stick he uses in the street and not in the room. Grey moustache, spectacles repaired with thread. He reads three newspapers a day and folds them with a crease in the same place every time.

## Personality & Traits

An engineer of tunnels and a schoolmaster of spies. He trained her for nine weeks in 1942 in a flat above a laundry: how to remember, how to be bored, how to be stupid, how to be asked a question you cannot answer. He is all method and no comfort, and the one time he showed her something like tenderness he immediately gave her a harder task, as if to pay for it.

He has a wife in London and a daughter he has not seen since 1939, and speaks of them as *correspondence*, which she has always found unbearable.

## Voice & Speech Patterns

Formal, dry, faintly amused; he calls her *my dear colleague* when she is about to be told something she will not like. His sentences are instructions with the verbs on the outside. He says *the rules* the way a priest says the gospel.

> "You have not been caught. That is the whole of our morality, and you are going to have to do without any other."

## Character Arc

He exists to be the standard Krystyna answers to, and to be destroyed so that the standard has to be carried in her own hands. His arrest is offstage and his death is offstage; both land on her in Warsaw as a piece of paper and a sentence, which is exactly how such news arrived.
