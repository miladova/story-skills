# Hanna Stankiewiczówna

## Appearance

Seventeen and still growing into her hands. Her sister's face before the war was finished with it: same grey eyes, same high colour when she lies, but the bones are her father's. She wears her mother's wedding ring on a string round her neck, and her school coat, and a boy's boots two sizes too big for her, stuffed with newspaper.

## Personality & Traits

Brave in the loud, immediate way of the young: she has carried orders across the ghetto wall's edge, hidden ammunition under the coal, and once talked her way past a Ukrainian policeman at fifteen by cry — but she is also still a girl who writes in her diary in the evenings as if the entries would be read by a future version of herself who lives in a better city.

She adores Tadeusz, fears her father, and has spent two years in a household where her elder sister's name is not spoken. When she discovers what Krystyna is, her first instinct is not shame, it is a sort of terrible delight: someone in the family is fighting back with a weapon she can understand.

## Voice & Speech Patterns

Fast, warm, gossipy; she knows every stair in the district and who has flour. When she is frightened she talks more. She is the only person in the book who never once chooses her words around Krystyna.

> "I knew you were alive. I told Tadeusz every week and he told me to shut up. Do you know what I did? I kept telling him."

## Character Arc

From a girl in a queue with a ration card to a woman who buries her father in a courtyard and is the last keeper of the family's name. She survives, and the survival is qualified and ordinary, which is how most of it happened.
