# Krystyna Stankiewiczówna

## Appearance

Twenty-four. Not tall — five foot three in the boots she has learned to walk in, which the German officers find enchanting and her brother finds obscene. Dark blonde hair with the last of its summer light in it, worn in the rolls and curls of 1943. Grey eyes that do the work: at a German dinner table they go soft and amused; on a Warsaw street they take in a doorway, a face, a poster, in half a second. She has her mother's hands (narrow, quick) and a small white scar on the left wrist from a stirrup, from the last summer at Wierzbów.

Under the fur, the body is a working instrument and she treats it as one: cold baths, no breakfast, a discipline of washing that is half cleanliness and half ritual, because after every night with von Hagen she has to walk into a bathroom and come out as someone who was never there.

## Personality & Traits

A professional liar with a dangerous private honesty. She can be charming in three languages and is fluent in a fourth, the one she never speaks: the stiff, formal Polish of her father's house. She is ruthless in a way that costs her; every cruelty is metabolised rather than absorbed, and the debt sits in her chest like a swallowed stone.

She loves her family with the guilt of a survivor. She loves Warsaw the way you love a person who is dying. She has no religious faith and prays anyway, in taxis and stairwells, with no expectation of an answer.

**Strengths:** Memory (trained, not natural); reading a room; a body that refuses to flinch; patience
**Flaws:** Contempt for the men she uses, including the ones she likes; cannot ask for help; believes that what she did in September 1939 is unforgivable, and so is willing to pay any price to be useful
**Blind spot:** She thinks of love as a liability to be managed, and cannot see that she has already spent part of herself on it

## Motivations & Goals

**Primary:** Get the information out. Whatever it costs, whatever it does to her.
**Internal:** To be worth the life she was allowed to keep
**Fear:** Being seen through by someone she loves — or worse, being seen accurately
**Want:** A room with a door she can close, in a country that exists again

## Voice & Speech Patterns

To Germans: warm, slow, a little foolish; grammatical German with an Austrian colour, and a Viennese shrug in her sentence endings. To Poles who know what she does: clipped, ironic, almost military. To Phillip: unarmoured, and therefore frightening. She answers questions with questions when she is buying time, and she uses her own name as a weapon exactly once, in chapter 23.

> "A woman would have done it better. You've always said women have no head for the long game. So whoever it is must be a man."

## Character Arc

**Starting state:** A weapon that works, keeping its own account in the dark.
**End state:** A woman who has paid for everything she has, has kept the information flowing to the end, and has chosen to live in a burnt city with the one man who knows all her names.

Flat arc, not static: the world changes her circumstances, and every chapter tests the same lie — that she can be two people and only one of them matters. The two POV men in her life are the two ways of being seen; the book's argument is that being seen is not the same as being known, and that she deserves both.

## Voice Notes For Drafting

- She notices Germans' shoes, hands, and the way they hold a cigarette: it is how she profiles them.
- Her interiority is unsentimental and short. When she is moved (her father's hands, Phillip's voice in Polish), the prose does not comment; it gives a physical detail and moves.
- Polish words in her mouth are for the moments she cannot afford them.
- She does not narrate her own attractiveness; she narrates it as a material, like gunpowder.
