# Tadeusz Stankiewicz

## Appearance

Twenty-eight, built like a middleweight who works in an office: thick neck, careful hands (he was going to be a lawyer), a grey Home Army sweater under a stolen German greatcoat. A broken front tooth he does not have fixed. He shaves on Sundays and looks older by Tuesday.

## Personality & Traits

An angry man with a system. He is a Home Army warrant officer in a sabotage cell — he has derailed things, burned things, killed a man with a length of pipe in a stairwell in 1943 and does not talk about it — and he runs his own family the same way: security above affection, no unnecessary words. He drinks too much on Fridays with the boys from his unit and is quiet by Sunday, which is when he goes to see his father and pretends to be well fed.

He loves his sister with an anger that has no valve. He cannot make the two facts meet — that she is doing something necessary, and that she is doing it in a German's bed — and the reader should never be allowed to settle it for him.

## Motivations & Goals

**Primary:** Keep his father and his little sister alive to the end of the war.
**Internal:** Be forgiven by someone for not being there in September 1939
**Fear:** That he will die before his father is safe

## Voice & Speech Patterns

Blunt, barracks Polish, black humour, orders to his own family. He swears heroically. When he is moved, he changes the subject by describing something practical: the quality of the boots he has just stolen.

> "You smell of their cigarettes, and you want me to admire your nerve. I do. Now wash, and don't touch the curtain, the neighbours count."

## Character Arc

He carries the burden of being the moral centre and the most difficult man in the room, and dies carrying a stretcher he could have dropped, which is the truest thing about him.
