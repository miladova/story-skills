# Zofia Stankiewiczowa

## Appearance

Fifty-four in 1939, tall, still handsome, dressed in the remade clothes of an earlier decade with a dignity that made servants cry. Grey hair in a bun. Grey eyes that went straight through people, and the same eyes in her daughter's face, which is why men believe Krystyna when she lies and why her father cannot look at her.

## Personality & Traits

The class and the manners of a house that had money and the instincts of a woman who has been living on less than it looks like since 1932. She is the one who made her daughter's languages (French from a Mademoiselle, German from a year in Dresden), her manners, her ability to be in a room and hold it. She is also the one who told her, on the platform in September 1939, to go and not look back, and then held her husband's arm so that he would not call her.

She dies offstage in 1941, before the book begins, and the book's only direct view of her is the platform and one scene at the well at Wierzbów where she watches her daughter with the German boy across the orchard and says nothing.
