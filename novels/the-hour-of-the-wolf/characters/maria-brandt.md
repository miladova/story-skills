# Maria Brandt

## Appearance

Fifty-eight, slight, hair in a knot, in the dark clothes she has worn since 1939. She keeps a small tin of good tea for guests and a photograph of her wedding day in Cologne facing the wall — she turned it once, in 1940, and has never explained.

## Personality & Traits

A Polish schoolteacher who married a German engineer in 1916 and has spent the rest of her life teaching in Polish to children whose parents asked her, politely, to stand a little further away. She is the reason her son speaks Polish like a kitchen and why his German is a schoolmaster's. She is pious, unyielding in small things, and appalling in a crisis, in the way of women who have buried three pregnancies and one brother.

She never touches the Volksliste question. She simply refuses to sign, and because she is Maria Brandt and the officials were embarrassed, they let her be, which is a kind of grace the book should not over-explain.

## Voice & Speech Patterns

Precise, with an old-fashioned Polish politeness; calls her son *synku* only when no German is in the room. When frightened she prays in Latin.
