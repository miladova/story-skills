# Iga Zaleska

## Appearance

Thirty, compact, hard-bodied in the way of a woman who has been living on coffee and cigarettes for two years. Dark hair cut short, cut by herself, badly. A face that is handsome in daylight and severe in a doorway. She dresses as a minor clerk — the Underground's favourite look — and carries a canvas satchel that is heavier than a clerk's satchel should be.

## Personality & Traits

The institutional spine of the book. She is not cruel and would be insulted to be thought so, which is different: she is a professional of security with a specialist's narrowness, and she looks at Krystyna with the flat assessment of a locksmith looking at a key of unknown provenance. She maintains the ledger of names, refuses to spend them, and sleeps four hours a night.

She has no interest in whether the arrangement is moral and every interest in whether it is safe. Nevertheless she is the one who goes to Tadeusz and forces him to keep silent — and later, in the Old Town, she is the one who tells Krystyna that in a siege all of them are whores, and that the word has been retired.

## Voice & Speech Patterns

Economical, unadorned, no humour at all. She gives instructions and waits for them to be repeated back. When emotion arrives — twice in the book — the sentences get shorter, not longer.

> "I won't pretend to like it. I will pretend to be glad you're alive, because that one's true."

## Character Arc

From distrust to something that is not friendship and is more use: respect, entered in a ledger like everything else. She survives, and in 1945 she is working in a records office, and the epilogue is the only chapter in which she smiles.
