# Emil Rutz

## Appearance

Forty-one, from a village near Bamberg. Heavy in the shoulders and soft in the middle; a face like a friendly shovel; hair cut with the company clippers. He keeps his uniform pressed and his boots oiled and his hands washed, and he is the only German in the book whose hands are clean in the literal sense.

## Personality & Traits

An ordinary man inside an evil institution, drawn without contempt. He is not political. His opinions are about roads, weather, the quality of the wine he is told to pour, and the photographs of his niece that he keeps behind the windscreen of the Mercedes. He is deferential to officers and warm to her in a domestic, unexamined way — he calls her *Fräulein Marion*, he puts hot water bottles in her car seat in winter, and he has never once asked her a question about Poland.

His one decision is the book's smallest and one of its heaviest. He salutes her because she is a lady leaving a house, and then he lies to his colonel, and is shot in a courtyard in Wola for it before the first week of August is over. Nobody in the story ever learns it but the reader.

## Voice & Speech Patterns

Simple German, country proverbs, mild complaint as small talk. He says *so ist es* about everything he cannot change, until the one morning he does not say it.
