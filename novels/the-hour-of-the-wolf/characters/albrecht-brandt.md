# Albrecht Brandt

## Appearance

Sixty-one, big-bodied and gone soft in the shoulders, in a fur-lined leather coat that was a rich man's coat in 1938. White hair cut by his wife; a moustache; hands that have been in machines all his life and have the black in the creases to prove it. He speaks Polish with a Köln accent and German with a Polish one, a man whose voice belongs to no country and is understood in both.

## Personality & Traits

A liberal bourgeois German of the old sort — reads the Frankfurter Zeitung, despises the Nazis in the way of a man who despises bad engineering — who nevertheless signed the Deutsche Volksliste in 1940 to keep the family workshop out of German receivership, and has never forgiven himself. He is honest, unimaginative, decent, and complicit: the book's most ordinary German and the one whose murder weighs the most, because he was murdered for nothing at all.

He loves his son and cannot look at his uniform. He loves Warsaw and was carried out of Wola to be buried by strangers.

## Voice & Speech Patterns

Slow, warm, technical; explains things; interrupts himself to check whether the other person knows a word. He asks his son about the weather because he cannot ask him about anything else.

> "Warsaw is a machine without oil just now. It will seize. I would like it to seize after I am gone, that's all. A small request, to the management."
