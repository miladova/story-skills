# Phillip Brandt

## Appearance

Twenty-six. Tall and slightly stooped, the stoop of a man who has spent his adult life leaning over documents and doorways he was not invited into. Fair hair cut short and ordinary; grey-blue eyes; the kind of unemphatic good looks that make him invisible in a room of uniforms, which is exactly how he has survived. He has a translator's hands, ink-edged, and a voice that carries the Polish of a Warsaw childhood and the German of a Hamburg grammar school.

He looks correct in the uniform. That is the horror of him. There is no softness in the shoulders that would mark him as unsuited to it, and Warsaw reads him the way Warsaw reads all of them: as the enemy.

## Personality & Traits

An interpreter by temperament before he was one by profession. He restates what other people say until it is exact, and he can hear the difference between what a German means and what a German says. He is fastidious, decent in small ways that cost him nothing, and complicit in large ways that he has stopped counting — until her.

He is not brave in the way the Poles on the barricades are brave. He is brave in the way a man with no other way of being honest is brave: quietly, repeatedly, and with terrible arithmetic about risk. He never declares anything. He arrives, he hands over what he has, and he stays standing.

**Strengths:** Languages; patience; a memory for documents; an unglamorous nerve
**Flaws:** Moral tidiness (he wants his betrayal of the Reich to be clean); too careful with her, and therefore slow; a temper at the bottom of the well that frightens him
**Nothing:** He has no wife, no children, no mistress. He has a mother in Wola, a father who signed a paper, and a photograph of a girl at a well in 1939 that he never destroyed.

## Motivations & Goals

**Primary:** To be one of the people who did something, rather than only one of the people who knew.
**Internal:** To be worth Krystyna Stankiewiczówna — the girl he has been in love with since 1938 and the woman he has to learn from scratch
**Fear:** That he will flinch, and that she will see it

## Voice & Speech Patterns

Precise, unhurried, constantly clarifying — his habit is to say a thing, and then say it again in a better order. In German he is clipped and correct; in Polish warm and slightly ungrammatical in the way of a house where a German father and a Polish mother argued at the table; in French, serviceable, with a Strasbourg accent that helps him in Paris and amuses her.

He asks small questions that reveal large attention: *How is your sister's cough? Which doorway did you use?* When he is frightened he becomes more precise, not louder.

> "I can give you the disposition of every searchlight on the Vistula. I cannot give you the assurance that I am worth your trust. That one, you will have to take."

## Character Arc

**Starting state:** An Abwehr analyst who has begun to flinch at his own files and believes that his hands are clean.
**End state:** A man who has fought in a Polish uniform without a uniform, killed for her, buried his father, and walked out of Warsaw under a false name with a limp and a Home Army card in his boot.

His change is not from cowardice to courage but from tidiness to mess: from keeping his hands clean to understanding that everything he now has was bought with his hands dirty.

## Voice Notes For Drafting

- His POV chapters (11, 20, 25) are cooler and more analytic than hers; he hears rooms as transcripts.
- When he lies, he does it well and hates it. When he tells the truth, he gives one more detail than is safe.
- His love for her appears as logistics: a route, a coat, a change of papers.
