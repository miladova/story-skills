# Premise-Reader Audit (October 2026)

**Target reader:** someone who has read only the premise (the synopsis in `story.md`),
was intrigued, and now opens the novel with no other knowledge — no character files,
no scene records, no planning documents.

**Method:** a reader-knowledge pass over the whole book. For every named person,
object and codename, the audit found (a) where the reader first meets it, and
(b) whether its identity/role is established on the page at or before the point
where the story starts leaning on it. Premise facts counted as *available* but
still required in-text delivery; planning-only facts counted as *not delivered*.

## Result

Of 25 audited subjects, 23 are properly delivered — introduced in-scene, by name,
with role and relationship attached (self-introductions, formal introductions,
family history, rehearsals of identity, and one memorable case of a character
introducing herself *and* explaining her false name in the same breath).

Two gaps were found, both the same failure pattern: a fact fully documented in
the planning layer but only whispered on the page. Both are fixed.

## Edits made (3 lines changed in total; +36 words)

1. **Chapter 1** — the Colonel now uses the cover name himself once, in the
   middle of the night conversation. Previously his dialogue never named her
   at all, so the reader had no signal about which name he believes is hers
   during the chapter's central scenes.
2. **Chapter 1** — at the dawn gate, the cover name is now anchored in one
   inserted clause: it is the name the household knows her by, the only name
   the Colonel has ever had for her. Previously the name appeared once,
   three-quarters through the chapter, from a minor character, unanchored.
   Chapter 2's papers/rehearsal material now reads as documentation of a
   fact the reader already holds, instead of a late reorganisation of
   Chapter 1.
3. **Chapter 12** — a Warsaw contact who was established across Chapters 9–10
   only by title, spectacles and behaviour acquires his surname in Chapter 12
   with no link back. The surname is now tied, in one clause, to the figure
   the reader already knows ("the Major, the man of the spectacles who had
   laid down the law to her across that cellar in March").

## Checked and deliberately left alone

- A codename that exists only in planning documents and never in the prose:
  the reader never needs it; inventing a use for it would be decoration.
- A one-word codename in Chapter 1 whose *substance* is explained in the very
  next lines: tradecraft colour, not a comprehension gap.
- Characters named once and identified in the following paragraph, POV
  chapters that name their viewpoint within the first paragraph, and objects
  whose plantings (Chapter 6) precede their payoffs by many chapters.
