# The letter he wrote in September 1939

## Setup

In chapter 6, on the last afternoon before Warsaw's surrender, Phillip writes a letter and gives it to the maid at the Stankiewicz house, because Jerzy has just thrown him into the street and the door will not open again. In the same chapter, Krystyna tells the reader - in her own head, briefly, and without weight - that the maid was dismissed in October and that no letter ever reached her.

## Payoff

In chapter 22, in a cellar under the Old Town, an old woman who used to work in that kitchen tells her what happened to it: the maid kept it in her prayer book through two years of hunger and gave it to the Home Army's post in 1942, where it went into a file, and the file was burned in a fire on Śliska Street in the first week of the Rising. All that survives of it is what the old woman remembers of its first line, which she recites. It is not the sentence anyone in the reader's company wanted it to be; it is better, because it is a practical sentence about a road out of the city, which is what he was.

## Tracking Notes

Never quote the letter in full. One line, remembered by the wrong person, four years late.
