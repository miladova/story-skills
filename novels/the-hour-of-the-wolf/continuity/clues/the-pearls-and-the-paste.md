# The pearls and the paste

## Clue

The Colonel gives her a strand of graduated pearls in chapter 1 with a particular pride: he can quote the price, the jeweller and the year. In chapter 13 she pawns them to get a dead courier's family out of Warsaw, and replaces them with paste bought at the Kercelak market for a tenth of the value.

## Planting Plan

Chapter 1: the necklace is a leash and a document of ownership, and the reader should be told the price, in francs, because a man like von Hagen tells you the price.

## Payoff Plan

Chapter 23, in his study, in the middle of the last night, he reaches for the clasp and knows. He does not say *these are false*. He says *You sold them*, and the entirely correct inference he makes from that is worse than the theft: a woman who sells a gift like that is a woman with a cause.

## Tracking Notes

Two mentions in between: the clasp at her throat in chapter 4, and the pawn ticket in her glove in chapter 13.
