# Faith and Ideology

## Setup

Two children from the same village took opposite vows in the war: she joined the People's Guard to build a just Poland, he joined the AK and then the priesthood. Each believes the other chose the wrong absolute.

## Rising Action

1. Halina defends the Party's works while knowing they are lies; Janusz defends the Church while knowing it sheltered men like him.
2. Each is forced to name what the absolute cost: her, a man's life; him, three lives and a hideout.
3. The village and the grave make the abstractions concrete.

## Climax

In Brzóza, at her brother's grave and under the birch tree, they stop arguing about which faith was right and begin the harder work of forgiving what each actually did.

## Resolution

Neither converts. She does not believe; he does not stop being a priest. They choose fidelity over agreement — a love that takes the form of Thursday tea, letters, and a bell neither of them can stop hearing.
