# Halina Sowa

## Appearance

Thirty-six in 1958. A lean, straight-backed woman who dresses in gray suits and black coats cut well and worn long. Her hair is short and severe, cropped with a knife in 1943 and kept short ever since. Her face is handsome rather than pretty, and she has learned to keep it still. She has her mother's hands. She wears no jewellery but a watch.

## Personality & Traits

Shrewd, patient, an excellent listener who gives away nothing. A writer of reports by trade and a reader of rooms by instinct. She is intimate with fear and has made a working peace with compromise; that peace is what the story breaks. She is capable of tenderness and deeply suspicious of it in herself. Her humour is dry and arrives at the worst possible moments.

## Backstory

Born 1922 in Brzóza, Lublin province, daughter of a landless farmhand and a strong, devout mother. In love with Janusz Wilga from fifteen. Joined the People's Guard in 1941 under the organiser Marek; took the name Sowa (owl). Learned of her brother Tadek's death in August 1943 and believed Janusz had betrayed the hideout; cut off her braid, stopped believing in God, and became hard. In February 1945, working in the press department of the Lublin security office, she was made by Rydel to sign a report naming Janusz's commander, Wiktor Berezowski, who was interrogated and shot. She has carried it for thirteen years and risen to deputy head of the Propaganda and Agitation Department of the PZPR Central Committee.

## Motivations & Goals

Wants to keep her position, her flat, and her usefulness; needs to be a person who can be looked in the face. The conflict is the engine of the book: every promotion has been bought with the 1945 report, and every day she does not confess is another day the machine owns her.

## Voice & Speech Patterns

Compressed, declarative, allergic to sentiment. Says the true thing in the flat voice of a woman reading a file.

- "That is not a reason. It is an answer."
- "I am not asking you to save me. I am asking you not to sign."
- "There is no answer to that which will not make you angrier than you are."

## Character Arc

- **Starting state:** A competent, guarded functionary who believes she has made her peace with the file in the drawer.
- **Key turning points:** The parish hall confession (ch3); locking the truthful report and sending the whitewash (ch4); the flashback that returns her to the village (ch5); telling the widow (ch9); the Control Commission and the loss of the flat (ch9).
- **Ending state:** Expelled, poor, working as a clinic bookkeeper, planning to teach in Brzóza in 1960. Free, and living with what she did.
