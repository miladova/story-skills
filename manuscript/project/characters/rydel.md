# Rydel

## Appearance

Late forties. A soft, heavy man with soft hands, soft voice, and heavy-lidded eyes. Gray suit, chain-smoker, always slightly apologetic. Looks like a professor of something harmless.

## Personality & Traits

Not a sadist. A believer in the machine, and in the necessity of dirty work. He is genuinely fond of Halina and genuinely prepared to destroy her. His weakness is his daughter, whom he loves and cannot lie to successfully.

## Backstory

Lublin security office, 1945, where he made the twenty-three-year-old Halina sign the report on Berezowski. Now an officer of the Security Service with an office on Rakowiecka Street and a say in whether episcopal appointments are approved. Widowed; raising Agnieszka, twelve.

## Motivations & Goals

Wants to keep the balance between a Church he does not believe in and a Party he does not trust, at the lowest cost in blood. Wants his daughter to go on believing in something.

## Voice & Speech Patterns

Reasonable, patient, faintly weary; asks real questions and then uses the answers.

- "This is not a trap, Father. It is a door."
- "I am not a monster. I am trying to protect this country."
- "The paper is not always right."

## Character Arc

- **Starting state:** The man with the file and the timetable.
- **Key turning points:** Janusz's refusal; the sermon printed on page four; Halina's statement; the Christmas Eve conversation in the car.
- **Ending state:** Still in the machine; did not send the statement up; a man who has lost the thing that made him certain.
