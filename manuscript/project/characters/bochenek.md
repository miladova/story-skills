# Zygmunt Bochenek

## Appearance

Forty-five, thick-set and well-fed, in good cloth; a man who has never been cold in his life. Turns his hat in his hands while he talks.

## Personality & Traits

A pragmatist and a builder, not a cynic and not a saint. He has kept parishes open under pressure, built churches with state money, and has decided that the Church survives by being useful rather than by being heroic. He knows exactly what he is and does not pretend otherwise.

## Backstory

Parish priest of Nowy Dwór for eleven years; a rival candidate for the Warsaw auxiliary see; appointed when the state refuses Janusz.

## Motivations & Goals

To hold the diocese together in the lean years, to build, and to be forgiven for the things that costs.

## Voice & Speech Patterns

Smooth, reasonable, and unexpectedly honest at the end of a conversation.

- "Do you think you are being used?"
- "I would rather it were you than me."
- "When I am consecrated, will you pray for me? Not for the mitre. For the thing the mitre is supposed to be."

## Character Arc

- **Starting state:** The state's man.
- **Key turning points:** The antechamber conversation; the visit to the rectory with the parcel of books.
- **Ending state:** A good bishop who will never say a costly thing, and knows it.
