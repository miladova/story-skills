# Wanda Kowalik

## Appearance

Thirties, worn, strong hands, red from laundry work; a headscarf at the door.

## Personality & Traits

Proud, unsentimental, funny in a dry way, ferociously protective of her children. She is the novel's testing ground for Halina's honesty.

## Backstory

Wife of Brunon Kowalik, the crane operator killed in October 1958 when a frayed cable snapped. Three children: Ewa (12), Marek (9), Tomek (6). Gave Halina Brunon's June letter.

## Motivations & Goals

To feed her children and to have her husband's death acknowledged as a death, not an accident.

## Voice & Speech Patterns

Plain, sharp, kind underneath.

- "He was a fool. But he was my fool."
- "Thank you for the bread."
- "He said it was dangerous. He said it could kill someone. It killed him."

## Character Arc

- **Starting state:** A widow being managed by the state.
- **Key turning points:** Giving Halina the letter; the whitewash report; Halina's Saturdays with the children.
- **Ending state:** Still poor, still strong, with a friend who comes on Saturdays.
