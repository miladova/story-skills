## Description

A village at the bend of a slow brown river in the Lublin uplands: one road, poplars, a co-operative, a school where the manor used to be, a ruined Jewish mill on the bank, and above it all the church of St. Michael with its cracked bell. Snow in winter, dust in summer, and the smell of the river everywhere.

## History

Manor, church, mill; the occupation from 1939; the deportation of the village's Jews in October 1942; the raid on the hideout in August 1943; the Germans burning the manor and the mill in July 1944; collectivisation after the war. Aniela Kowal hid a Jewish girl in the attic for two weeks in 1942 and was beaten for it.

## Culture & Customs

Sunday Mass, the Angelus three times a day, the autumn harvest, the wafer at Wigilia, the dead visited on All Souls'. The old people say "the dead don't vote," and the young people leave.

## Notable Features

The birch tree by the river where two children first kissed; the cemetery on the hill; the mill; the bell with the crack in its waist.

## Current State

1958: a model collective with a new tractor and a photograph of Gomułka in the school lobby, and beneath it the same poverty as before the war.
